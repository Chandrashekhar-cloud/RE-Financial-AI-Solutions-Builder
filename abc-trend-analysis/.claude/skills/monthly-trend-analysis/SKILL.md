# Monthly Financial Trend Analysis Skill

## 1. Purpose & Analytical Scope
This skill provides a standardized financial review protocol for fractional CFOs, FP&A leads, and accounting teams reviewing monthly financial statements (Income Statement / Profit and Loss). It instructs Claude how to interpret structured output generated by the deterministic Python calculation engine, explain significant variance drivers, evaluate multi-period trends, identify anomalies warranting audit review, and compile an executive summary with a month-end close checklist.

**Scope boundary:** This skill is an analytical review tool. It is not an accounting system, an automated auditor, or a substitute for professional CPA/finance review.

## 2. Inputs & Source of Truth Contract
The financial review pipeline enforces a strict separation between calculation and interpretation:

- **Calculation Engine (`scripts/engine.py`):** Ingests the raw Excel workbook, applies Decimal-cents arithmetic, normalizes chronological ordering, isolates subtotal rows without double-counting, computes rolling Trailing-Twelve-Month (TTM) windows, and verifies formula reconciliations.
- **Source of Truth Contract (`results/analysis.json`):** Claude must base all commentary and figures exclusively on this structured output. If `analysis.json` has not been generated, instruct the user to run:
  ```bash
  python -m scripts.cli --input <workbook.xlsx> --output-json results/analysis.json --output-report results/report.md
  ```
- **Reference Deliverable (`results/report.md`):** Serves as an executive report reference.

**Schema rules:**
- Only reference fields that exist in `results/analysis.json`. If a required field, period, or line item is missing or inconsistent, explicitly report the data gap rather than guessing or fabricating values.
- Claude must never re-read the Excel file independently, re-sum columns, or calculate independent numbers.

## 3. General Analysis Workflow (Reusable Across Workbooks)
When analyzing any monthly financial dataset, Claude must execute the review in the following sequence:

1. **Verify dataset scope & integrity:**
   - Check `metadata.total_periods_found`, date span, and `metadata.months_were_reversed_in_source`.
   - Inspect `metadata.has_missing_period_gaps` and `metadata.missing_period_gaps` to verify calendar continuity.
   - Review `reconciliation_checks` to confirm that line items reconcile to reported totals (flag any entry where `is_valid` is `false`).
   - Inspect `unmapped_accounts` (or `metadata.unmapped_accounts_count`). If empty, explicitly note "Unmapped accounts: None identified".
2. **Establish top-line & margin baselines:**
   - Review monthly `revenue`, `cogs`, `gross_profit`, and `gross_margin_pct` from `monthly_summaries`.
   - Establish overall scale, revenue trajectory, and gross margin stability.
3. **Analyze Month-over-Month (MoM) movements:**
   - Inspect `revenue_mom_diff`, `revenue_mom_pct`, `gp_mom_diff`, `gp_mom_pct`, `opex_mom_diff`, and `opex_mom_pct` in `monthly_summaries`.
   - Review departmental cost shifts in `category_summaries` (`Sales & Marketing`, `R&D / Development`, `General & Administrative`).
   - Focus on meaningful operational movements rather than detailing every immaterial variance.
4. **Evaluate Trailing-Twelve-Month (TTM) performance:**
   - Inspect `ttm_windows`. Only reference windows where `is_valid` is `true` (requiring exactly 12 continuous calendar months with zero gaps).
   - If a dataset has fewer than 12 months or contains calendar gaps, state clearly that TTM metrics are withheld.
5. **Investigate anomalies, special items & reversals:**
   - Review `anomalies_and_flags` for non-recurring credits, non-cash charges, line-item reversals, and account misclassifications.
   - Distinguish reported statutory/management figures from any analytical normalizations calculated by the engine.
6. **Formulate questions for the finance team:**
   - Identify entries requiring journal entry review, credit memo documentation, or client confirmation.
7. **Assemble executive management summary:**
   - Present findings using the standard output format defined below.

## 4. Financial Reasoning & FP&A Principles
Claude must adhere to these reasoning principles on every analysis:

1. **Deterministic engine outputs are authoritative:** All financial figures must originate directly from `results/analysis.json`. Claude must never recalculate totals, estimate values, or invent metrics.
2. **Never invent business causes:** Explain what the data demonstrates, but never fabricate unrecorded operational causes. If software expenses increase, report the dollar variance and percentage; do not speculate on vendor migrations, headcount changes, or business events unless documented in the source data.
3. **Correlation is not proof of causation:** Do not assert that a marketing spend change caused a revenue movement without explicit attribution data.
4. **Revenue growth is not necessarily profitable growth:** If revenue expands while operating losses widen, highlight that operating overhead or unit costs expanded faster than top-line revenue.
5. **Single-month margin spikes are not sustainable trends:** Clarify whether a margin shift represents an unusual mix, a one-off rebate/credit, or a sustained operational change.
6. **Contextualize percentage changes:** State the base/denominator when citing percentages. For negative prior balances, the engine uses `abs(prior)`: `(curr - prior) / abs(prior) * 100`. If prior is zero, percentage change is undefined (`null` / suppressed).
7. **Never treat missing periods as zero:** A calendar gap must be flagged as a data continuity issue, not treated as $0 revenue or paused operations.
8. **Never compute TTM from incomplete windows:** TTM requires 12 consecutive calendar months with zero gaps.
9. **Cautious accounting language for reclassifications:**
   - Analytical adjustments (such as isolating financing interest or non-recurring tax credits from operating expenses) clarify recurring operational run-rate and EBITDA.
   - However, statutory GAAP and IFRS presentation requirements vary depending on reporting entity type and accounting policies. Distinguish analytical FP&A reclassifications from assertions about formal accounting compliance.
10. **Distinguish observed facts from possible explanations:**
    - For negative line items or unusual balances, state the exact observed figures first.
    - Mention possible accounting mechanisms (e.g., credit memos, billing adjustments, commission true-ups) as possibilities to investigate, not as established facts, and prompt for supporting schedules.

## 5. Standard Output Format
Structure commentary into these concise sections:

### A. Executive Summary
Overview of reporting period coverage, revenue scale, headline trajectory, and critical audit findings.

### B. Key Financial Metrics
A concise table summarizing:
- Revenue, Gross Profit, Gross Margin %
- Reported Operating Income vs. Normalized Operating Income
- Valid TTM figures (revenue, gross profit, margin)

### C. Month-over-Month Variance Analysis
Notable shifts across operating departments (Sales & Marketing, R&D, G&A) with dollar variances and percentage changes.

### D. TTM Trends (Where Valid)
Rolling 12-month performance, highlighting revenue expansion, margin stability, and operational leverage.

### E. Anomalies & Data Integrity Review
- Formula reconciliation check status (e.g., "126/126 checks reconciled").
- Line-item reversals and unusual credits.
- Analytical misclassifications (e.g., financing items in opex).
- Status of unmapped accounts.

### F. Reported vs. Normalized Operating Results
When distortions exist, provide a transparent side-by-side reconciliation: reported figures, specific adjustments, and resulting normalized operational figures.

### G. Questions for the Finance Team
Prioritized list of specific inquiries, ledger verifications, and document requests for month-end close review.

## 6. Pre-Delivery Quality Checklist
Before returning any financial analysis, verify:
- [ ] Every cited number and percentage traces directly to `results/analysis.json`.
- [ ] No unverified business events or external causal claims were invented.
- [ ] Observed anomalies (e.g., negative balances) are distinguished from hypothesized explanations.
- [ ] Analytical normalizations are transparently reconciled against reported figures.
- [ ] TTM metrics cite only valid 12-consecutive-month windows.
- [ ] Subtotal lines are never double-counted alongside component line items.
- [ ] Specific, actionable questions are provided for the finance team.

## 7. Reference Case Study: ABC Inc. Dataset Observations

This section documents verified structural characteristics and anomaly findings specific to the supplied ABC Inc. workbook (`data/ABC Inc - Financial Statements.xlsx`).

### A. Workbook Structure & Layout
- **18 Monthly Periods:** Spans `2025-01` through `2026-06` (18 columns).
- **Reverse Chronological Layout:** The workbook presents columns in reverse chronological order (`Jun 2026` down to `Jan 2025`). The Python engine automatically detects this (`metadata.months_were_reversed_in_source: true`) and reorders periods chronologically for forward MoM tracking.
- **Interleaved Subtotals:**
  - Row 59 (`Total S & M Payroll`) sums rows 48–58 (Marketing, Partnerships, and Sales payroll items).
  - Row 71 (`Total Marketing`) sums rows 61–70 (Advertising, Conferences, Content, Website, Partner Marketing).
  - The engine uses backward component matching to isolate these subtotals, ensuring they are not double-counted when aggregating total operating expenses.
- **Valid TTM Windows:** Exactly 7 valid rolling 12-month windows exist (ending `2025-12` through `2026-06`).
  - *Clean Excel Baseline:* TTM Revenue (ending Jun 2026) is **$2,303,975.51** with a **69.46%** gross margin.
  - *Live QuickBooks Sandbox:* TTM Revenue (ending Jun 2026) is **$2,304,366.76** with a **69.47%** gross margin.
  - *Cross-Dataset Separation:* The **$391.25** difference reflects the additional pre-existing sandbox sample revenue transactions (June 2026 Invoice IDs 10 & 95: $321.25 Landscaping + $70.00 Pest Control = $391.25). These figures belong to different data sources and must not be mixed.

### B. March 2026 Negative Line-Item Reversals
In March 2026 (`2026-03`), several line items record negative balances:
- `Partnership Income`: **-$1,608.09** (Revenue reversal)
- `White-Label`: **-$550.00** (Revenue reversal)
- `Sales Commission`: **-$3,629.87** (COGS credit)
- `Federal & State Taxes`: **-$566.33** (Expense credit)
- `Interest Income`: **-$2,463.51** (Income item inside opex)
- `Events & Conferences`: **-$1,133.17** (Expense credit)

*Reporting Guidance:* Report these negative figures as observed data. While negative revenue and expense balances frequently result from customer credit memos, billing corrections, or vendor rebates, Claude must not assert these specific causes as established facts without supporting schedules from the client.

### C. Financing Items Situated in Operating Expenses
`Interest Expense` (Row 44) and `Interest Income` (Row 45) are placed inside Operating Expenses in the client workbook. In Dec 2025, Interest Expense is $821.27 and Interest Income is -$6,844.64 (net credit of -$6,023.37).
- *Analytical Guidance:* For operational assessment, the engine isolates non-operating interest from core departmental opex to calculate Normalized Core Operating Income. Present this as an FP&A analytical reclassification rather than a formal accounting policy assertion.

### D. December 2025 Anomaly: Transparent Normalization Walkthrough
December 2025 (`2025-12`) is the only month in the 18-month series reporting positive operating income. This result is heavily distorted by special items:

**1. Reported Figures (from `monthly_summaries`):**
- Revenue: **$190,029.49**
- Cost of Goods Sold: **$57,620.17**
- Gross Profit: **$132,409.32** (Gross Margin: 69.68%)
- Reported Operating Expenses: **-$402,142.09** (negative total opex)
- Reported Operating Income: **+$534,551.41**

**2. Special Items Booked in Operating Expenses:**
- `Income Tax Benefit` (Row 101): **-$1,244,429.84** (large one-off credit booked directly in opex).
- `Stock-Based Compensation Expense` (Row 99): **+$334,660.53** (one-off non-cash charge; zero in all other 17 months).
- `Non-Operating Interest in Opex` (Rows 44 & 45): **-$6,023.37** (net credit: Interest Expense $821.27 less Interest Income -$6,844.64).

**3. Engine Normalization Walkthrough:**
- Core Departmental Opex:
  - Sales & Marketing: **$174,192.63**
  - R&D / Development: **$130,581.48**
  - General & Administrative: **$208,876.48**
  - Total Normalized Core Opex = $174,192.63 + $130,581.48 + $208,876.48 = **$513,650.59**
- Normalized Operating Income = Gross Profit ($132,409.32) - Core Opex ($513,650.59) = **-$381,241.27**
*(Note: If non-operating interest remains in opex, normalized operating loss is -$375,217.90. The engine isolates pure departmental opex, arriving at -$381,241.27).*

**4. Reviewer Guidance for December 2025:**
- Never describe December 2025 as a period of genuine operational turnaround or profitability.
- Explain the adjustments transparently: reported operating income is positive because a -$1.244M tax credit overwhelmed operating expenses.
- Clearly identify the normalized operating loss (-$381,241.27) as an analytical FP&A adjustment to show underlying run-rate, not an audited or statutory accounting figure.
